# Round 1 Viability Assessment (Austin themed cafés)

Evidence quality warning: most cost and margin figures come from commercial estimator or vendor sites (low quality). Competition facts come from local news (moderate). Scores are judgment calls built on that evidence. "Inference" is marked where used.

## Shared evidence
- Austin startup cost for a plain coffee shop is estimated at about $66k-$336k (build-out $16k-$80k, equipment $15k-$45k). Permits run from a few hundred dollars to about $2k, and a lease-to-open timeline of 3-6 months is typical. Evidence quality: low (aggregators). https://startcosts.com/coffee-shop/texas ; https://howmuchtostartabusiness.com/compare/coffee-shop/austin-tx/houston-tx ; https://costofcity.pages.dev/cities/austin/start-a-coffee-shop
- Austin Public Health and City of Austin requirements were not found directly. Verify the food establishment permit, certificate of occupancy and fire approvals with the city.
- Coffee is already dense and trending (Austin was named best US coffee city in 2026, per the ideas file).

## Per-concept findings

### 4. Roll for Roast (board game café), 7/10
- Demand (inference): UT students, a large tech workforce, and the sober-social trend. Competition from other board game cafés was not searched in depth. This is a gap in the evidence, so check before committing.
- Cost: moderate. A game library, shelving and ordinary café build-out. No special licensing was found (inference). Alcohol-free keeps TABC out of scope.
- Margin: vendor models say 5-15% net, with COGS of 25-35%. Table or cover fees help cover staffing. Evidence quality: low (model vendors). https://finmodelslab.com/blogs/how-much-makes/family-friendly-board-game-cafe
- Seasonality: indoor, so it is heat-proof. The UT calendar causes summer dips, but family and adult leagues offset them (inference).
- Website fit: excellent. Weekly league night, house rules (clean hands, respect the pieces, ask before joining), and a menu of drinks and snacks.

### 6. Botanica Tea & Matcha Conservatory, 6/10
- Demand is real: Mori Matcha has queues, and La La Land is expanding to The Domain by Sept 2026. Competition is intensifying, and Brooms Matcha closed in Aug 2025, which suggests the segment is crowded and volatile. Sources: https://www.axios.com/local/austin/2026/03/05/austin-pop-up-fuels-matcha-craze ; https://whatnow.com/austin/restaurants/handmade-matcha-concept-plans-to-reopen-on-south-lamar/
- A greenhouse in Texas heat means high cooling cost and plant upkeep (inference).
- Margin potential on matcha and tea is good (inference). Ingredient costs for ceremonial matcha are high.
- Website fit: very good, with a Sunday plant swap and photogenic content.

### 8. The Study Hall (focus and maker café), 6/10
- Many student study spots already exist (Medici, Bennu 24h, Epoch, Houndstooth). Source: https://theculturetrip.com/articles/the-10-best-coffee-shops-near-the-university-of-texas
- Bookable pods need extra build-out. Maker workshops need instructors and supplies. Revenue is lumpy around the UT semester (inference).
- Differentiation (silent zones, workshops) is clear, and the website fit is good (weekly workshop night, quiet-hours rules).

### 2. Cartridge & Cup (arcade café), 5/10
- Established competitors: Pinballz (very large, 300+ games), Cidercade (150+ games, flat admission). Source: https://www.kineticist.co/news/play-pinball-austin-texas
- High capex for cabinets and repair is a heavy ongoing burden (inference). Any Texas coin-operated amusement registration or licensing was not verified.
- Late-hours sober nightlife is a differentiated angle. Website fit is good (league night, rules).

### 3. Paws & Pour (dog yard café), 5/10
- Proven demand and existing competitors: BARk House Social, Yard Bar, Neighbors Dog Park & Cafe, and Mutts Canine Cantina (site plan approved 2021). Source: https://communityimpact.com/austin/north-central-austin/business/2025/08/28/off-leash-dog-park-bark-house-social-serves-food-drinks-in-east-austin
- Needs land or a large patio lot, fencing, a waste system and liability insurance. Texas heat limits yard use for much of the year (inference). The permit path for dog park plus food was not found.
- Website fit: good (weekly training class, rules about vaccinations and leash handling).

### 5. Little Longhorn Lab (family play café), 5/10
- The gap looks real, since searches found little competition (Austin-specific coverage was thin, so the gap may reflect missing data).
- Play-café sellers cite admission as the main revenue stream, and some Texas play centers report strong revenue, but these are seller claims. Source: https://www.softplay.com/blog/play-cafe-profits/
- Child care licensing question was unresolved (depends on whether you supervise children). Insurance and safety compliance are heavier than for other concepts. Verify with Texas HHSC.
- Weekly story time suits the website, and the rules are easy to write.

### 7. Static & Steam (vinyl and live-music café), 4/10
- Austin venues face rent pressure and closures (Red River district, Holy Mountain, Cheer Up Charlie's). Source: https://www.axios.com/local/austin/2026/09/14/red-river-cultural-district-venue-closures-austin
- Live music adds noise and zoning risk, plus a crowded field of free live music (inference). Nightly sets add staffing cost with a low cover charge.
- Website fit: good (weekly open mic, rules for quiet during sets).

### 1. The Bat Cave Café, 4/10
- The bat season runs about March to November, with a colony of 750k-1.6M and about 100k visitors a year claimed. Sources: https://www.lonelyplanet.com/articles/guide-to-bats-in-austin-texas ; https://dallas.culturemap.com/news/travel/03-25-19-guide-to-bats-austin-congress-avenue-bridge-time-season
- A near-bridge downtown location has high rent. The concept is tourist-driven and seasonal, with a weak base of repeat local customers (inference). Charcoal and butterfly-pea drinks are a gimmick.
- Website fit: fine (nightly bat watch), but it is a weak recurring-customer model.
